What to Wear for Your Portrait Session: A Simple Guide That Actually Works

The wardrobe question comes up in almost every consultation, and it's a good one to ask. What you wear doesn't make or break your portraits, but it does shape the mood. Here's how I think about it with every client.

Solid colours, almost always.

Busy patterns compete for attention in a portrait. Stripes, large florals, graphic prints — they pull the eye away from your face, which is the whole point of a portrait. Solid colours keep the focus where it belongs. When in doubt, reach for something simple.

Tones that work with studio light.

My studio is built around soft, neutral tones. The colours that tend to photograph most beautifully in this setting are warm whites, cream, dusty rose, sage, caramel, and deep earth tones. Rich jewel tones — burgundy, forest green, navy — also look striking if you want something with more presence. What tends to fight the image rather than complement it: neon, large colour-blocked graphics, or anything with text or logos.

Layers add depth.

A blazer, a cardigan, an open shirt over a simple base — layers give a portrait something to work with. They add visual texture and let us shift the look mid-session without a full outfit change. Even one extra layer is worth bringing.

Think about how you want to feel.

This matters more than the outfit itself. If you feel most like yourself in something elegant and simple, wear that. If you feel most comfortable in something relaxed and lived-in, bring that. The best portraits aren't created by the right outfit — they're created when you feel settled in what you're wearing.

A few things worth leaving at home.

Anything that pinches, pulls, or that you'll be adjusting throughout the session. Clothes that are slightly too small. Anything you're wearing to look a certain way rather than because it feels right. The camera picks up discomfort quickly, and no outfit is worth that.

You don't need to figure it out alone.

Before every session we talk through what you're planning to wear. Bring options if you're unsure — two or three different looks gives us something to choose from and often leads to a more varied gallery. And if wardrobe is the thing making you hesitant to book at all, that's exactly what the consultation is for.

When you're ready to talk through your vision, schedule a complimentary consultation. It's just a conversation.
